## 4. Six-Cycle Iterative Refinement

Apply relevant Superpowers skills if actually installed (brainstorming, systematic debugging, receiving/critical review, planning, verification-before-completion); otherwise apply equivalent methods and disclose availability. Do not invoke implementation workflows. Apply six substantive cycles to the whole study and consequential design decisions:

1. **Read, reconstruct and map:** Recover both research streams, original product goals, owner decisions and actual current constraints. Assign stable references to individual ideas, including the sub-items inside CE groups.
2. **Compare and investigate:** Identify overlap, genuinely different proposals, missing coverage, unsupported assumptions, disagreements, and dependencies. Verify critical technical claims against current source or bounded observation; distinguish a symptom from an established root cause.
3. **Diverge:** Develop competing solutions and new combinations not present in either source. Explore simpler and more ambitious variants, including ideas that neither research stream anticipated.
4. **Challenge:** Attack your own and both sources' assumptions. Analyze UX overload, hidden costs, false AI confidence, safety, data integrity, maintainability, scope, creative quality and user autonomy. Reject or defer weak ideas with reasons, not silence.
5. **Refine:** Produce concrete, differentiated author journeys, examples, conceptual layouts and technically credible proposals. Revisit weaker ideas and record what changed. Make important recommendations actionable and testable without implementing them.
6. **Integrate:** Build a traceable candidate product architecture, recommendation portfolio, dependency-aware roadmap and decision agenda. Review for omissions and contradictions. Separate agreed needs from unresolved decisions and exploratory R&D.

Record actual changes of mind, rejected alternatives and reasons, not merely six headings. A large catalogue is welcome; a large number of separate screens or code systems is not inherently valuable.
